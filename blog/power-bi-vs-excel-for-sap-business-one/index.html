Power BI & Excel

Power BI or Excel for SAP Business One Reporting?

It's rarely a choice between the two. Excel and Power BI answer different questions for different people. Here's how to decide which reports belong where.

Rishad Ahmed · November 1, 2026 · 3 min read

When a business on SAP Business One outgrows exporting reports by hand, the next question is usually: "Should we move to Power BI, or stay in Excel?"

My honest answer is that most distributors and manufacturers need both, built on the same data. The trick is knowing which reports belong in which tool, so you don't pay for dashboards nobody opens or keep rebuilding spreadsheets every Monday.

Where Excel Wins

Excel is still the best tool for the people who work with the numbers every day: buyers, collections, customer service and accounting.

  • Everyone already knows it. No training, no new logins.
  • It's built for working lists. An open-order list, a buy-now list or an AR aging that someone filters, sorts and annotates is a natural fit for a spreadsheet.
  • It can refresh itself. A workbook connected to SAP through Power Query pulls fresh data when it opens, with no copy-paste and no exports.
  • It can arrive by email. With Power Automate, a refreshed report can land in someone's inbox every morning.

Excel struggles when a report has to combine large volumes of history, be shared with many people with different access, or be viewed on a phone.

Where Power BI Wins

Power BI is built for the people who need to see the business at a glance: owners, managers and sales leadership.

  • Trends and comparisons. Year-to-date versus last year, margin by customer, sales by rep: interactive and visual, with a click to drill into detail.
  • Large history. Years of invoice lines from SAP, including old and new databases after a migration, in one model.
  • One version of the truth. Everyone opens the same dashboard instead of their own copy of a spreadsheet.
  • Security by person. Row-level security can show each sales rep only their own customers, from the same report.
  • Phone and tablet. The Power BI app works well for checking numbers on the go.

Two things to plan for: sharing reports with colleagues requires a Power BI licence for each viewer, and because SAP Business One usually runs on your own server, scheduled refresh needs Microsoft's on-premises data gateway set up once by IT.

A Simple Way to Decide

If the report is…Use
A working list someone acts on (open orders, buy-now list, AR aging)Excel
Checked daily by one team, then filtered and editedExcel
A trend, comparison or KPI for owners and managersPower BI
Years of history, or more than one SAP databasePower BI
Shared with many people who should each see only their partPower BI
Needed on a phonePower BI

The Setup That Works: One Data Layer, Two Tools

The mistake I see most is building Excel reports and Power BI dashboards separately, each with its own logic for what "sales" means. Within months they disagree, and nobody trusts either.

The better setup is to define the numbers once: a set of SQL views or queries on SAP that handle cancelled documents, credit memos, freight and currency correctly. Then both tools read from that same layer:

  • Excel workbooks for the teams who work the lists.
  • Power BI dashboards for the people who steer the business.
  • A reconciliation check that ties both back to SAP's own Sales and Purchasing Analysis.

Change a rule in one place, and every report follows.

Where to Start

If you're starting from scratch, don't buy licences first. Start with the two or three reports people rebuild by hand most often, and automate those in Excel. Once the numbers are trusted, add a Power BI dashboard for the owner on top of the same data. You get value in weeks, and every report agrees from day one.

Rule of thumb: if someone needs to act on rows, use Excel. If someone needs to decide from a trend, use Power BI. If both are true, build the data once and use both.
